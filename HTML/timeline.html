THE ROAD TO FREEDOM

From resistance to freedom.

India's independence was not achieved in a single moment. It was shaped by generations of resistance, movements, sacrifices and an unbreakable desire to be free.

A NATION'S STORY

Every year carried a chapter.

Follow the moments that changed India's political landscape, brought millions into the freedom movement, and ultimately transformed a colony into an independent nation.

12 EVENTS

1857
THE FIRST GREAT UPRISING
EARLY RESISTANCE

The Revolt of 1857

A major uprising against British East India Company rule erupted across northern and central India.

The revolt began among Indian soldiers in the East India Company's army and quickly spread to several regions.

Rani Lakshmibai Bahadur Shah Zafar Nana Sahib Tantia Tope
02
1885
THE RISE OF ORGANIZED NATIONALISM
NATIONAL MOVEMENT

Birth of the Indian National Congress

A national political platform was created for Indians to express their political demands.

Founded in Bombay in 1885, the Indian National Congress initially focused on constitutional reform, political representation and greater Indian participation in administration.

A. O. Hume Dadabhai Naoroji W. C. Bonnerjee
03
1905
SWADESHI & SELF-RELIANCE
NATIONAL MOVEMENT

Partition of Bengal

The partition of Bengal triggered widespread protests and gave rise to the Swadeshi Movement.

Indians boycotted British goods, promoted Indian-made products and encouraged national education and economic self-reliance.

Bal Gangadhar Tilak Bipin Chandra Pal Lala Lajpat Rai Aurobindo Ghose
04
1915
A NEW ERA OF LEADERSHIP
MASS MOVEMENT

Gandhi Returns to India

After years of activism in South Africa, Mahatma Gandhi returned to India.

His experience with Satyagraha and non-violent resistance in South Africa shaped his approach to India's freedom movement.

Mahatma Gandhi Raj Kumar Shukla Vallabhbhai Patel
05
1919
A TURNING POINT
TURNING POINT

Jallianwala Bagh

On 13 April 1919, British troops opened fire on a gathering at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar.

The massacre shocked the country and generated widespread anger against British rule.

Mahatma Gandhi Reginald Dyer Rabindranath Tagore
06
1920
MASS NON-COOPERATION
MASS MOVEMENT

Non-Cooperation Movement

Gandhi encouraged Indians to withdraw cooperation from British institutions.

People boycotted government schools, colleges, courts, titles and British goods. The movement brought millions into active participation.

Mahatma Gandhi Ali Brothers C. R. Das Motilal Nehru
07
1930
THE SALT MARCH
MASS MOVEMENT

Dandi March

Gandhi's Salt March became one of the most recognizable acts of civil disobedience.

Beginning on 12 March 1930, Gandhi marched from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi. On 6 April he broke the salt law by producing salt.

Mahatma Gandhi Sarojini Naidu Abbas Tyabji C. Rajagopalachari
08
1931
NEGOTIATION & POLITICS
POLITICAL NEGOTIATION

Gandhi-Irwin Pact

Gandhi and Viceroy Lord Irwin entered negotiations during a crucial period of the movement.

The agreement included the release of many political prisoners and temporarily ended the Civil Disobedience Movement.

Mahatma Gandhi Lord Irwin
09
1935
CONSTITUTIONAL CHANGE
CONSTITUTIONAL DEVELOPMENT

Government of India Act

A major constitutional reform introduced expanded provincial autonomy.

The Act created provincial legislatures with increased powers. Indian political parties later participated in provincial elections in 1937.

Indian National Congress British Government
10
1942
DO OR DIE
MASS MOVEMENT

Quit India Movement

India demanded an immediate end to British rule in August 1942.

Gandhi called for the British to leave India. Major leaders were arrested, but protests, strikes and underground resistance continued.

“Do or Die.”
Mahatma Gandhi Jawaharlal Nehru Aruna Asaf Ali Usha Mehta
11
1945–46
THE FINAL YEARS
FINAL YEARS

INA Trials & Naval Uprising

The end of World War II brought new political pressure on British rule.

INA officers associated with Subhas Chandra Bose were tried at the Red Fort. Public sympathy for the INA spread widely.

In 1946, unrest among Royal Indian Navy ratings spread from Bombay to other locations.

Subhas Chandra Bose Shah Nawaz Khan Prem Kumar Sahgal Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon
1947
15 AUGUST 1947
FREEDOM

Independence

After decades of struggle, India became an independent nation on 15 August 1947.

The Indian Independence Act created the independent dominions of India and Pakistan.

At midnight between 14 and 15 August, Jawaharlal Nehru delivered his famous Tryst with Destiny speech.

Jawaharlal Nehru Mahatma Gandhi Sardar Patel Dr. B. R. Ambedkar
15 AUGUST 1947
1947

The struggle became freedom.

“At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom.”
— Jawaharlal Nehru, Tryst with Destiny

India's independence was shaped by generations of resistance, political movements, sacrifices, negotiations, and mass participation.

The story did not end in 1947. Freedom became a responsibility to carry forward.
